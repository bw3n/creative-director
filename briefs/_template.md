## Original brief

Verbatim or close paraphrase. Preserve the brief's own language where it matters.

## Agent's first read

The output of the brief-critique job. Five or six bullets: restate, human truth, tension, cliché, gaps, cheapest read.

## Corpus cards loaded

List the campaign cards, category cards, brand cards, and principle cards the agent pulled for this brief. Filenames only. This is the load-order record.

## Work done

The output of the actual job (ideation, art direction, critique, etc.). Quote the agent's output verbatim or close paraphrase.

## Your decisions

What you kept, what you rejected, what you changed. The brief card is the journal of your taste — what gets kept is more useful than what gets made.

## Output

Anything produced for the client: deck, KV direction, brief rewrite, presentation. Filename or one-line summary.

## Outcome

Did the work ship? Was it killed? Did it run? If known, what was the result? This field is often empty — most briefs don't reach market. Fill it when you can.

## Lessons

One or two bullets. What did this brief teach the corpus? Did it produce a new principle, a new category, a new brand card? Did it confirm or contradict an existing principle?

The lessons field is the bridge from brief history back to corpus growth. Every brief with a non-empty lessons field contributes to the next iteration of the corpus.
