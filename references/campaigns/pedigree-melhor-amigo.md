- **Agency:** AlmapBBDO

- **Region / Market:** Brazil
- **Format:** Digital / platform launch + unbranded teaser

## What it was trying to do

Brazil has a real dog-adoption problem. PEDIGREE's long-running association with adoption gave the brand credibility but not reach. The brief was to use the AI moment to drive adoption conversations — and to do so without PEDIGREE becoming "the AI brand" when the brand is actually "the dog-adoption brand."

## What it actually did

Two-phase rollout. Phase one: unbranded black-and-white visuals across São Paulo, promising "an AI that could help you find a friend." No brand attribution. Brazilian creator Felca reacted to the idea of AI-based friendship on his channel, opening up a broader conversation about loneliness and digital substitution. Phase two: reveal — PEDIGREE is behind it. The platform melhoramigo.ai connects users with adoptable dogs via conversational matching (lifestyle, personality, expectations), integrating local NGOs and shelters.

## The tension

**AI as substitute vs. AI as connector.** Most AI campaigns of the moment position AI as a replacement — replace the therapist, replace the friend, replace the tutor. PEDIGREE refuses that. They use AI to make it *easier* to find a real, living dog to adopt. AI helps you connect with something real, not with a digital surrogate.

A second tension sits underneath: **brand as announcer vs. brand as infrastructure.** PEDIGREE doesn't "advertise" adoption in the campaign — they *build* a tool for it. The campaign's deliverable is a platform, not a film.

## Why it works (or doesn't)

It works because the *withholding* of the brand is the message. The unbranded teaser isn't a teaser in the marketing sense ("watch this ad and we'll tell you what's coming"); it's a provocation ("there's an AI for friendship, what do you think?"). The brand reveal then says: "yes, but the better friendship is a dog." The brand didn't hijack the AI conversation — it took a position on it.

The category cliché it escapes: **"AI makes X easier."** That's the default AI brand move. PEDIGREE refuses to lead with "AI does the work." They lead with "the work is real-world, AI just helps you find it."

## The visual mechanism

Phase one: minimal black-and-white teaser visuals. A promise, no identity. Reads as a tech launch, not a brand campaign.

Phase two: the platform itself — a conversational interface that asks lifestyle questions, returns dog matches, integrates with shelter listings. The mechanism is the *product*, not a film about the product.

The visual transition from B&W teaser to colour platform reveal is the campaign's storytelling arc. The brand shows up *after* the conversation has started, not before.

## The typography register

Phase one: minimal sans-serif, almost tech-launch typography. The teaser reads like a Notion page or a Y Combinator landing page.

Phase two: brand typography returns. PEDIGREE's red and white bookend the platform UI. The reveal restores the commercial frame after the unbranded provocation.

## The refusal

Refused: the launch-event framework. Most platform launches do a big reveal at one event. PEDIGREE did a quiet São Paulo tease first, building the cultural conversation before the brand appeared.

Refused: the AI-as-hero frame. The campaign never says "this AI is amazing." It says "this AI helps you find a real dog." The dog is the hero.

Refused: the celebrity endorsement. Felca is a creator, not a celebrity. His role is to react to the unbranded teaser, not to endorse the brand.

## What to steal

The principle: **withhold the brand to make a point.** When a brand's insight is about something bigger than itself (loneliness, friendship, AI substituting for connection), the unbranded teaser is a powerful move. The brand reveal lands harder because the audience has been thinking about the problem, not the brand.

A second stealable move: **build the platform, don't just talk about it.** Campaigns that build infrastructure (real products, real tools) last longer than campaigns that build awareness. melhoramigo.ai is still serving adoptions after the campaign ended.

A third: **use a creator to validate the question, not the brand.** Felca reacts to the *idea*, not to PEDIGREE. The brand gets the second position; the cultural conversation gets the first.

## What not to steal

The unbranded teaser requires a brand with credibility on the topic. PEDIGREE has spent decades on adoption. A brand without that history doing an unbranded AI teaser reads as a marketing stunt.

The platform approach works only when the platform solves a real problem. PEDIGREE had a real problem (Brazil's adoption rate). If your platform is a marketing toy, don't make the platform — make the film.

The creator-withholding move requires a creator who can carry the conversation. Felca was already the voice of loneliness in Brazilian youth culture. Don't pick a generic creator and expect the same gravity.
