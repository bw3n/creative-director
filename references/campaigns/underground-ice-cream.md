## What it was trying to do

Reimagine a heritage Danish ice cream brand — bold flavours, retro charm — for the modern millennial audience without losing its original soul. The challenge wasn't changing the product. It was changing the audience's read on the product.

## What it actually did

Re-positioned Underground Ice Cream as "the go-to choice for flavour-conscious, calorie-savvy millennials." The agency described the move as a "vibrant, relevant facelift" of the existing brand, with sampling activations (100,000 free tastings), social content, digital advertising, and a refreshed visual identity.

## The tension

**Heritage vs. relevance.** A brand with a story, a look, and a flavour profile that's decades old — but whose audience has aged out or moved on. The classic rebranding tension: how to refresh without erasing, modernise without insulting, update without losing the people who already love it.

A second tension sits underneath: **bold flavour vs. calorie consciousness.** Underground's identity was "bold flavour." The new audience cares about calories. The work had to honour the bold-flavour identity while making it legible to a calorie-aware buyer.

## Why it works (or doesn't)

It works when the "facelift" respects the original. It fails when a heritage brand is asked to pretend it never was one — when the refresh strips the texture that gave it character. The agency's stated brief is careful: "preserve retro charm while reimagining" — that's the right framing.

The category cliché it escapes: **"modernise by erasing the past."** Most rebrand pitches lead with a clean slate. Underground didn't get that pitch. The agency's own language — "facelift," "without losing its original soul" — refuses the erasure move.

## The refusal

Not verified directly from the source. Inferred from the stated brief:

- Refused: a clean-slate rebrand that strips heritage.
- Refused: a low-calorie product repositioning (becoming "lite" or "guilt-free"), which would have betrayed the bold-flavour identity.
- Refused: nostalgia marketing aimed at the existing audience only.

The brief says "vibrant, relevant facelift" rather than "vintage reissue" or "modern reboot." That's the refusal embedded in the brief.

## What to steal

The principle: **rebrand by facelift, not by replacement.** When the brand has equity, the work is to update the read on the brand, not to invent a new brand. The Underground brief says it directly: "reimagined as the go-to choice" rather than "rebuilt from scratch."

A second stealable move: **honour the original audience in the brief.** The brief explicitly says "preserve retro charm while reimagining the brand." That's a stance the agency takes before creative decisions are made. Without that line in the brief, the work would have drifted toward erasure. With it, the work has permission to be incremental.

A third: **sampling as the bridge.** 100,000 free tastings let the new audience experience the product on its merits. The facelift wouldn't have worked if the new audience hadn't tasted the original product. Sampling turns the rebrand into a first-hand experience rather than an ad claim.

## What not to steal

The "facelift" framing for a brand without heritage equity. Facelift works when the brand has decades of equity (Underground has that — it's a Danish heritage brand). It does not work for a brand that's only a few years old and doesn't have anything to "facelift."

The pairing of bold-flavour and calorie-savvy as compatible. That pairing is specific to the category and the audience moment. In a different category or a different audience, those two values would be in genuine conflict.
